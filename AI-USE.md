# Use of AI-Assisted Tools

This document is the canonical, repository-level disclosure of how AI-assisted
tools were used in developing The Stewardship Standard stack (QSM, TSS, THRIVE,
QSM-FAI). The same disclosure appears in the front matter of each specification.
It follows emerging best practice for AI disclosure in academic and
open-standards work: state plainly *how* and *where* AI was involved, affirm
human accountability, and do not overstate or understate the tools' role.

## Summary

- **Author and accountable party:** Caitlin Stokes. The author defined the
  problem space, the core constructs, the domain ontology, and the design
  intent, and retains full responsibility and accountability for all content —
  normative and non-normative.
- **AI was used substantially**, under direct human supervision, including to
  draft and structure specification text (normative requirements and reference
  schemas), explore alternative framings, refine terminology, and check
  internal consistency.
- **AI is not an author.** Large language models are treated as tools. They
  cannot take responsibility for the work, respond to peer review, or hold
  authorship.
- **Human review of all output.** Every AI-generated artifact was reviewed,
  corrected, validated, or rejected by the author before inclusion.
- **No unverified claims.** AI was not relied upon as a source of empirical or
  factual claims, and citations were verified rather than accepted as generated.

## What AI was used for

- Drafting and structuring specification prose, including normative
  requirements, conformance rules, and explanatory text.
- Generating and formalizing the reference JSON schemas from normative object
  definitions.
- Exploring alternative framings, names, and organizational structures.
- Refining terminology and improving clarity and consistency.
- Cross-checking internal consistency across the four documents (version
  references, term usage, schema/spec alignment).

## What AI was *not* used for

- Bearing authorship or responsibility for the work.
- Originating the conceptual framework or its claims of novelty as
  finished, unreviewed contributions.
- Producing empirical results or factual/literature claims accepted without
  the author's independent verification.
- Generating citations treated as authoritative without verification.

## Why we disclose substantial use rather than minimal use

Much disclosure boilerplate limits AI to "improving non-normative explanatory
text." That would not be an accurate description of this project, where AI
assisted in drafting normative content and schemas. Accurate disclosure is the
ethical requirement; we therefore disclose the substantial role openly. This
does not diminish authorship: the conceptual ownership, direction, synthesis,
and final judgment are the author's, and the framework can be articulated and
defended independently of the tools used to express it.

## Provenance and human conceptual ownership

The framework was developed by the author across an extended period of
human-directed conceptual work prior to and throughout formalization. The
author maintains a private record of how the core concepts evolved (including
the direction given to AI tools), which substantiates the human steering role
and the originality of the synthesis. Priority and novelty rest on that
human synthesis and the specific design constraints chosen, not on any output a
generic model would produce for any user in the same domain.

## Accountability statement

All normative requirements, definitions, conformance criteria, and design
decisions in this standard are the responsibility of the named author. Errors,
omissions, and claims are the author's own.
